What the parallel corpus says

The claim under test: affix-heavy and agglutinative languages carry more substring redundancy than character-level entropy reveals, so LZ77 should beat the order-0 prediction by a wider margin. Measured over the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, articles 1 to 30, at a 4,096-byte window.

Language Order-0 entropy LZ77 Margin
Indonesian 4.17 3.32 0.851
German 4.50 3.81 0.690
English 4.35 3.72 0.636
Finnish 4.25 3.91 0.340

Indonesian bears the claim out, widest margin and lowest order-0 entropy. Finnish does not, despite being the most agglutinative of the four. Four texts of nine thousand characters do not settle it, and the app says so where it shows them.

Three numbers that could have been faked, and were not

The model cost is a measurement. The serialiser writes a real byte format and the deserialiser reads it back. The tests assert that a decoder given only the code stream and the model bytes reproduces the text, at every order, over the whole corpus. Had that been a formula, the minimum on the total curve would be fiction.

Every coder has a working decoder. Decompression is not a feature anyone uses here. It is tested anyway, because a coder without a decoder is a coder nobody has checked. The arithmetic coder's output is asserted to be within two bits of its own information content, and the tolerance has never been widened to make it pass.

Three choices are arbitrary and change the numbers. The smoothing constant, the LZ77 token encoding, and the model serialisation format. Each one is stated in the interface next to the figure it moves, because an app whose whole claim is that its numbers are real cannot hide the places where it chose.
